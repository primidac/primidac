primidac.com Redesign Playbook
By Claude, based on your 10 screenshots of primidac.com. Goal: turn the site into a minimalist portfolio for a product engineer working in cyber security and AI automation.
1. The big picture
The site you have today is well crafted. It has a distinctive identity: cream paper, ink outlines, chunky offset shadows, mono “//” labels and a terminal-log hero. That is more personality than most agency sites. But it presents a colorful product studio (“we”, clients, revenue), and you want to present one minimalist engineer who works in security and AI.
What works and should stay
• The cream and ink base, the mono “// LABEL” device, and the idea of a live code window.
• Primidac Labs. Building your own products is your strongest proof and your best differentiator.
• The pink “P” logo mark, which gives you a recognizable brand color.
What works against your goal
• The voice is agency-style (“we”, “your capital”, “Fortune 500”). There is no name and no face anywhere.
• Cyber security and AI automation are almost invisible. “AI” appears in a code sample and a chat widget, and security appears only in a boilerplate compliance section.
• Four colors rotate through every section (pink, yellow, mint, ink). Color is used as decoration, so it carries no meaning.
• Ten sections that mostly say the same thing: “we are rigorous”. Minimal sites say less, more clearly.
• Heavy styling everywhere: big shadows, tilted cards, grain, mascot illustration. That is the opposite of minimal.
The three changes that matter most
1. Change the voice from “we, the agency” to “I, the practitioner”.
2. Make security and AI visible in the first screen.
3. Subtract. Fewer sections, one accent color, quieter shadows.

3. Section by section
3.1 Navigation and the chat widget
Now: a floating pill nav with six links, a yellow “Book a Call” button and an unlabeled bell icon. A pink “Agent Blake / BETA ONLINE” chat pill sits bottom-right on every screen.
Problems
• Six top-level links is too many for a portfolio. Philosophy, FAQ and Products are not things a visitor looks for first.
• The bell has no label, so people can’t tell what it does.
• The chat pill competes with your main call to action on every screen. Its white text on pink has a contrast ratio of about 2.8:1, below the 4.5:1 minimum for readable text.
• “ONLINE” should only be shown if it is true.
Do this
• Nav: Work, About, Writing, Contact plus one button (“Book a call”). Move the newsletter to the footer or label the bell.
• If Agent Blake is a real AI-automation demo, make it a project in Selected work with a live demo. A floating widget undersells it.
• If you keep the widget, make it small, quiet and click-to-open. Say what it is (“AI assistant, beta”) and what it does with messages. For a security-focused portfolio, disclosure is part of credibility.
3.2 Hero
Now: a dark rounded panel with a blurred wireframe image and grain. The headline reads “Engineering Digital Products That Drive Revenue. Yours, and Ours.” with “Revenue.” in a pink box. Below it are a paragraph and two equally weighted buttons.
Problems
• It is the loudest thing on the page but says nothing about you. “Yours, and Ours” is ambiguous.
• The highlight spotlights “Revenue”, the least distinctive word on the screen.
• Dark panel, blur and grain make it heavy rather than minimal.
• Two buttons of equal weight split attention.
Do this
Keep the highlight device, but point it at what makes you different. Three options:
• A: “I build secure products and AI automations.”
• B: “Product engineer. Security-minded. Automation-obsessed.”
• C: “Software that ships, stays secure, and runs itself.”
Subline: “I’m [Your Name], a product engineer. I take ideas from prototype to production, harden them against real attackers, and automate the boring parts with AI. Currently building WorkCrayon and Primidac Network.”
Buttons: See my work (primary) and a plain text link “Book a call”. Replace the dark panel with a calm cream hero, or keep a panel with one sharp product screenshot instead of the blurred image.
3.3 Technical Foundation (code window and robot)
Now: “We build software that scales.” with a yellow marker underline, faded terminal log lines scattered behind, a robot illustration and an “AI.TS” window showing import { AIArchitecture } from '@primidac/core'.
Problems
• “Scales” is a claim without proof.
• The scattered log lines reduce readability.
• The robot is a generic mascot that could belong to any company.
• @primidac/core and AIArchitecture look like a real package. Security people will check. If it isn’t real, label it as an example.
Do this
Keep the idea, because a live code window is a great on-brand signal, but make it true. Show one window with a sanitized snippet from your own project, such as a server-side payment verification function or an agent workflow definition. Remove the robot and the floating logs. Suggested title: “Built to run in production.”
3.4 Built by Founders
Now: “Most agencies just write code and hand it over. We don’t.” followed by two paragraphs mentioning “skin in the game” and “ruthless efficiency”.
Problems: it defines you against agencies when you no longer want to be one. “Your capital” and “ruthless efficiency” sound aggressive. It is still “we”, and there is no face.
Do this: turn it into About. Photo on the left, three sentences on the right:
I’m [Your Name], a product engineer based in [city]. I build software end to end and care about two things most teams treat as extras: security and automation. I ship my own products at Primidac and run Primidac Network, a community of security and AI builders.
Add three facts: years of experience, your main stack, one proof point.
3.5 Technical Capabilities
Now: “Strategic architecture. Measurable ROI.” with three cards: Deep Strategy, Flawless Execution, Measurable Results (pink, yellow and mint icons).
Problems: abstract nouns that fit any agency. “Elite human strategy”, “premium brand positioning” and “qualified leads” are marketing language. The ROI is claimed but not shown.
Do this: replace with What I do, three plain lines that match your real skills:
• Product engineering: prototype to production across mobile, web and SaaS (React Native, Next.js, Node, Supabase).
• Security: threat modelling, hardening, and ethical testing on authorized targets.
• AI automation: agents and workflows that remove manual work.
Use small ink line icons or none at all.
3.6 Domain Expertise
Now: SaaS, FinTech and E-Commerce cards, with the third card tilted.
Problems: three industries with no example. “Compliant” and “don’t crash on Black Friday” are promises you would have to keep.
Do this: cut the section. Use SaaS, FinTech and the like as small tags inside case studies. If you have a real payments build (for example USDC-gated access with server-side verification), make it a case study.
3.7 Execution Protocol
Now: an alternating timeline with three phases (Deep Dive, Build, Launch & Scale), colored dots, and text about auditing “your brand” and “leaks in your funnel”.
Problems: this is agency process boilerplate about brand and funnels, not engineering. It takes two screens of scrolling to say three sentences.
Do this: compress it into one line or a three-step strip inside How I work: Understand, Build, Harden and ship.
3.8 Primidac Labs (WorkCrayon)
Now: “We build our own products.” with a WorkCrayon card (“IN BETA” tag, “Join the Waitlist”) and a “View Full Ecosystem” button.
Assessment: this is your strongest section. It proves you build. But the card holds a single vague sentence (“like no other”) and a waitlist button.
Do this
• Promote it to the top as Selected work, project 1.
• Add a real screenshot and a three-line case study: problem, what it does, stack. Replace “like no other” with one concrete claim.
• Add Primidac Network and Agent Blake as projects 2 and 3.
• Rename “View Full Ecosystem” to “All projects”.
• A case study template is in the appendix.
3.9 Methodology
Now: “Uncompromising Standards.” with Clean Architecture, Rigorous Testing and Performance Optimization.
Problems: absolute claims such as “bulletproof”, “millisecond load times” and “we don’t tolerate technical debt”. Security people avoid the word “bulletproof” because nothing is. It is also “we” again.
Do this: shrink to How I work with three practices a visitor can check, such as strict TypeScript, tests that run in CI before every deploy, and the Lighthouse score of this very site. Link to a repo or a CI badge as proof.
3.10 Security Posture
Now: “Institutional-Grade Security.” with Enterprise Compliance (“Architected to meet SOC2, HIPAA, and GDPR standards from day one”), Zero-Trust Security and Resilient Infrastructure.
This is the most important section to fix.
• “Fortune 500” and “Institutional-Grade” are claims you can’t prove.
• SOC2 is an audit result an organization earns. Software can’t “meet SOC2” on its own. HIPAA and GDPR compliance depend on how the whole organization operates, not just the code.
• For someone who wants credibility in cyber security, an overclaim is worse than saying nothing. Reviewers will notice, and it can create legal exposure.
Do this: rewrite as Security, in practice, with specifics you really do. Adopt only what is true:
• Every payment verified on the server, never trusted from the browser.
• Secrets in environment variables, never in code.
• Least-privilege roles and rate-limited endpoints.
• Dependency audits in CI.
• Security headers on this site (link to the securityheaders.com result) and a /.well-known/security.txt file.
If you did the design work, “designed with SOC2 and GDPR controls in mind” is honest. “Meets SOC2” is not.
This section is where your identity as a security person lives, so make it the second most prominent part of the page after Selected work.
3.11 Sections to add
• Selected work: three case studies, with screenshots.
• Writing: your three best notes or posts. This builds credibility and brings in search traffic.
• Contact: one email, your Calendly link and your socials. If you keep a form, keep it to three fields.
4. Color theory
4.1 What the site uses now
Approximate values sampled by eye from your screenshots (confirm with a color picker): paper cream #F2F0EA, ink #1B1B1B, coral pink #E87B8B, butter yellow #F6E15F, mint #B5F0D3, hero panel grey #3A3A3A.
The icons cycle through pink, yellow and mint in every section. That makes color decorative: it doesn’t tell the visitor anything. In a minimalist design, color should carry meaning.
4.2 Core principles
• 60-30-10: 60% paper, 30% ink and neutrals, 10% one accent.
• One accent only. Reserve it for the primary button, one highlighted key word per page, link hover, and status.
• Color is a signal, not a decoration. If you remove it and nothing is lost, it wasn’t doing a job.

4.4 Recommended palette
• Paper #F4F1EA: page background.
• Surface #FFFFFF: cards and panels.
• Ink #161616: text and borders.
• Muted #66665F: secondary text.
• Hairline ink at 12% opacity: dividers and card borders.
• Accent coral #E8788A: fills and underlines only, with ink text on top.
• Dark mode (optional): background #121212, text #F2EFE8, accent unchanged (coral on near-black passes comfortably).
Drop yellow and mint from the main palette. Use them only for status if you need them, for example an ink-outlined “IN BETA” tag instead of a yellow one.
4.5 Color psychology and the two-site system
• Coral and pink read as approachable and creative. That suits a product studio, but a security audience wants to see precision, so balance it with lots of neutral space and sharp typography.
• A cool accent (deep blue or green) feels more technical, but you would lose continuity with your pink logo mark. I recommend keeping coral and using it sparingly.
• Keep your two sites distinct but related. primidac.com is warm cream and coral: calm and human. Primidac Network is black and white: secretive and brutalist. The shared element is the node mark.
5. Style guide
Typography
• Keep your heavy grotesque display face, but drop the weight from 800 to 600 or 700 and tighten the width of headlines.
• Sizes: H1 clamp(2.2rem, 5vw, 4rem) (yours is currently about 5.5rem), H2 clamp(1.8rem, 3.5vw, 2.8rem), body 17 to 18px with 1.6 line height, 65 characters maximum line length.
• Use the mono face only for small labels, code and metadata. Keep the “// LABEL” device in ink, with the slashes in coral.
Layout and spacing
• Use an 8px spacing scale and section padding of clamp(80px, 12vw, 160px).
• Left-align long headings and text. Centered long headings read as marketing. Left-aligned ones read as editorial and engineering.
• Use an asymmetric grid on About and Writing: small label on the left, content on the right.
Shape and depth
• Corner radius about 12px (yours is about 28px).
• 1px hairline borders instead of thick outlines.
• Remove the chunky offset shadows. Keep one signature element at most, such as the primary button on hover.
• Remove tilted cards, and reduce the grain overlay to almost nothing or remove it.
Icons and imagery
• Replace the pastel icon tiles with thin ink line icons or nothing.
• Replace the robot and wireframe art with real things: screenshots in plain frames, a photo of you, real code.
Motion
• No loader.
• Hero text fades up over about 400ms.
• Scroll reveals happen once: 300 to 400ms ease-out, 24px travel at most, 60ms stagger.
• Hover: a link underline that slides in, and a button that moves 2px.
• One signature animation: the code or terminal window typing a real snippet.
• Animate only transform and opacity. No parallax. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Copy voice
Use first person, specific nouns and numbers. Avoid superlatives (“elite”, “flawless”, “bulletproof”, “ruthless”). Show instead of claim.
